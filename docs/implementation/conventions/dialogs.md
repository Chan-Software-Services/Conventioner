# Dialogs

**[`AppDialog.vue`](../../../front-end/src/components/AppDialog.vue) is what a dialog is in this product**: a native `<form>` in a modal doing one small job.
It owns the scrim, the window, the close control, Escape and backdrop dismissal, holding the page behind inert, focus on open, and the `type="submit"` confirm.
A new dialog is built on it, never hand-rolled; a panel that slides in from a side is [`SidePanel`](../../design-system.md#the-primitive-layer), through the same composables.
Not every modal is a dialog on it.
Three take the shell's parts rather than copying them: the [vendor drawer](../../business/flows/vendor-drawer.md), a panel beside the page; the door code's full screen ([`DoorCodeScreen.vue`](../../../front-end/src/components/DoorCodeScreen.vue), [attendance and the door](../modules/attendance-and-door.md)), which fills the window at the entrance where a dialog is a small window over the page; and [the app bar's sheet](../modules/app-bar.md#the-sheet-on-a-phone), a list of places hanging from the bar, which holds no form, title or actions for the shell to own and keeps the same contract with `useInertBehind`, `useEscapeToClose` and `useFocusGoesBack`.
The door code's screen declares `role="dialog"` and `aria-modal` itself, and makes it true with `useModalRoot`, `useEscapeToClose` and `useFocusGoesBack`, so the page behind is inert, Escape closes it while it is on top, and focus opens on its Close and goes back to "Door code"; its one confirmation, New code, is an `AppDialog`, teleported beside it as a sibling.
A window that declares `role="dialog"` itself stands on a `div`, as the vendor drawer's and `SidePanel`'s do: ARIA lets no `aside` take that role, which a screen reader may then announce as the landmark rather than the dialog, and [`rolesNotAllowed`](../../../front-end/e2e/helpers/allowedRoles.ts) asks axe's `aria-allowed-role` of each window alone, where it opens, a best practice the suite-wide check leaves out.

## The rules

**The form is the Enter contract.**
Enter in a field runs the same handler as the confirm button, so it inherits that handler's guard, and a disabled submit makes Enter inert with no key handler anywhere.
Do not add `@keydown.enter`: five views each invented their own answer, and one of them, the vendor's sign-in, bypassed its own resend cooldown by doing so.
One exception is a picker's open list ([`SearchPicker.vue`](../../../front-end/src/components/SearchPicker.vue), E38/F08/S03): while its list is open, Enter takes the highlighted choice, or closes the list when none is highlighted, and is kept from the form (with `multiple` the list stays open after a choice, so the next Enter is the list's too), and Escape closes the list and is kept from the dialog; once the list is closed both keys are the dialog's again, so Enter submits through the form's guard and Escape closes, except that under `holdsChoice`, while words cover the choice, the first Escape names the choice again and stays at the field.
The list stays through a press past it, until the press ends, so the click lands on the Save below it, which closing it as the focus goes would move away.
The other is a field that only narrows what is shown, as a [`ListFrame.vue`](../../../front-end/src/components/ListFrame.vue)'s find field and Export vendors' filters ([`ExportVendorsForm.vue`](../../../front-end/src/components/ExportVendorsForm.vue)) and column list ([`ColumnChecklist.vue`](../../../front-end/src/components/ColumnChecklist.vue)) have, in which Enter does nothing: it narrows the rows as it is typed in or ticked, so Enter there would only save what the person was looking through.
Export vendors holds it once on what wraps its filters, for an `input` of any kind, a tick and a combobox's closed field among them, and for a closed `select`, whose Enter, by the browsers' source, submits the form on a Mac, and leaves a button its key, so Enter still presses a toggle or a chip's Remove; and once on its column list, which holds only a find field and ticks.

**A dialog of several independent actions passes no `confirmLabel`** and hosts its own `<form>`s in its body; Manage organization has three.
Their submits are their own: the shell listens for `submit` only when its body is the form, so an inner form's submit neither is prevented by it nor makes it emit `submit`.
It still gets the scrim, window, close, Escape, backdrop and inert behaviour, without the single-form footer.
**Its own buttons can still be pinned at its foot, through the `actions` slot**: the footer draws with `confirmLabel` or that slot, and with the slot alone the body is no `<form>`, so those buttons submit nothing and an inner form's submit is still its own.

**A dialog is one of three widths, `size="narrow"` (26rem, the default), `"wide"` (38rem) or `"broad"` (60rem), never wider than the window less a 16px gutter at each side**, and never a percentage of the window.
Narrow holds two controls, wide a list, and broad a list beside what it gathers, the export dialog's order and preview.
A dialog's window is a rounded frame that never scrolls, so no scroll bar can square its corners; what is taller than it scrolls in a [`ScrollBox`](../../../front-end/src/components/ScrollBox.vue) ([the design language](../../design-system.md#the-primitive-layer)) inside it.
**By default that box holds the title and the body (`scroll="window"`)**, with the close staying put over it, on its own white ground, so words scrolling beneath it never run through it.
**The close stands on the title's first line** (T422, the QC walk of 2026-11-16): its middle on that line's middle, and its glyph as far in from the window's right edge as the title is from its left, 24px, in either scroll.
Its top is the window's 24px inset and half the title's line, `--text-lg` times [`--leading`](../../design-system.md#line-height), with the button moved up by half its height, so it follows the title's line if either token changes; the title sets its own `line-height: var(--leading)`, so a parent's line height cannot move the line from under it.
The walk found it 16.5px above the line and its glyph 20px in, in every dialog, since every dialog is this shell.
The button is 8px wider than its glyph on each side, so it stands 16px from the edge; on a phone `--target-min` makes it 44px wide, its glyph centred in it a few px further in, as the prototype Daniel approved has it.
A long title wraps short of the close, in either scroll: its lines end 64px from the window's right edge at a desk, clear of the close's 16px to about 54px, and 68px in at 640px or less, 8px short of the 44px close; a word too long for its line breaks rather than running under the close, as a role's name in "Show who" may.
The [vendor drawer](../../business/flows/vendor-drawer.md)'s close stands on its name's first line by the same rule, under the "Vendor details" eyebrow; the door code's full screen has no close beside a title, its Close a labelled button leading the row of controls above the poster, so the rule has no line there to stand it on.
This default stays for a short dialog, a question with a control or two, by Daniel's choice (T204's choice 21): only the long dialogs move to `scroll="body"`.
So a dialog on a page that fits a phone opens inside a 390px window, on each place [the design language](../../design-system.md#layout) lists as fitting one; only the people and invite rows of Manage market and Manage organization needed a phone rule of their own.
The footer's buttons wrap where they are wider than the window, as the placement dialog's Cancel, Free this seat and Move them are on a phone; a row that could not wrap ran out past the window's left edge, where nothing scrolls to it.

**A long dialog scrolls only its body, with `scroll="body"`** (T200): the title and close stay at the top and the error and actions at the bottom, and only the body scrolls, in a `ScrollBox` between them.
When the whole window scrolls, its title and Save leave the view.
The window takes the height its content needs, up to 80% of the window's height, Daniel's "sensible proportion of the view height" (T191 F02).
With nothing pinned at its foot the body runs to the frame, so its shade ends there rather than above a blank band, and its own end holds the window's 24px inset.
A `ScrollBox` draws no bar and shades an edge with more beyond it, `--edge-shade` over `--edge-shade-depth` from `base.css`, 12px by Daniel's choice 20; it measures as the body scrolls and as it or its content is resized.
On a browser that draws classic bars no bar lies over the body, which clipped the export dialog's column order on the right (T191 F03).
The body, the error and the actions are still the one `<form>`, so the Enter contract holds as it is.
**The shell draws every title through `EmailAddress`** (T315), so an address in it breaks only before its "@" or a dot, never just after a hyphen ([`EmailAddress.vue`](../../../front-end/src/components/EmailAddress.vue)); words with no address draw as the plain text they are, and the `title` string still names the dialog, since the pieces add nothing between them.
Manage organization's Remove, "Make member" and "Make admin" confirmations and Manage market's Remove and role change name a person's address in theirs; there is no `title` slot, since the string is all a title holds.
**What the dialog has gathered goes in its `tray` slot**, drawn only when given, between the body and the error and actions: in `scroll="body"` it is a `ScrollBox` of its own, pinned, that keeps its height up to 40% of the window (Daniel's choice 20), the body giving way, and scrolls inside itself past that; in `scroll="window"` it is in the flow after the body.
**At 640px wide or less, or 640px tall or less, a `scroll="body"` dialog is as tall as the window less the 24px gutter above and below**, whatever its content needs, so its pinned parts never crowd its body to nothing (Daniel's choice 22); the height clause is for a window split short, where 80% would leave the body nothing.
**The one exception is "Choose what leads the card", by Daniel's choice (T446): it passes `fitsContentOnAPhone`**, so there it is as tall as its content needs, up to the same cap, with its title and Done pinned and its ticks scrolling between them past the cap, since its list is usually short and a window-tall dialog left a band of white above Done.
No other dialog of the product passes it; the library's `dialog` entry shows it on a short list.
`dvh` sizes it, since a phone's browser bar would otherwise cover its pinned actions.
An `anchored` dialog keeps its top where it opened, under the same 80% cap; on a phone or in a short window it is as tall as what lies below that top less the gutter.
**A dialog is long when its body can outgrow a laptop window as a market's data grows**: its window scrolled in a sweep, or its body holds a list that grows with the market.
A long dialog says `scroll="body"`: Manage market and its Permissions, Manage organization and its Default permissions, Update info, the placement dialog and the assignment history, Delete organization, Fix the form, Open applications, Change reviewers and the whole list of reviewers, "Choose what leads the card" (which fits its content on a phone, as above), the reviewed applications, the role report's "Show who", Export vendors, a layout's "Use saved types", both confirmations of a permissions reset, and a role change's.
`scroll="window"` stays the default, for every other dialog: a question with a control or two, as "Leave without saving?" is ([unsaved work asks before it is dropped](unsaved-work-asks-before-it-is-dropped.md)), a narrow destructive confirmation mounted once in `App.vue` and opening on Stay.
The library's `dialog` entry shows each width, the window's scroll beside the body's on 150 people, the tray, a footer with no form, a list with nothing to focus, a short list that fits its content on a phone, and every behaviour below in either scroll, and its `export-vendors` entry a broad dialog with its whole tray, the shipped `ExportVendorsForm` on the library's market ([the component library](../modules/component-library.md)).
A list the server pages goes in that body as a [`PagedList.vue`](../../../front-end/src/components/PagedList.vue) with `box="parent"`, so the body is the one scroller, as [the assignment history](../../business/flows/placing-by-hand.md#j13-assignment-history)'s does.
A long list goes in that body as a [`ListFrame.vue`](../../../front-end/src/components/ListFrame.vue), whose field keeps its Enter, as above, and which needs nothing else there: One by one's reviewed applications are so, and the library's `summaries` entry shows it ([`the-component-library-summaries.spec.ts`](../../../front-end/e2e/the-component-library-summaries.spec.ts)).

**A dialog sits centred, unless its body grows as it is used**: `anchored` holds its top where it opened, so a field's error appearing as a key is typed does not move the dialog half a line under the pointer (the amendment's dialog, E38/F02), and Export vendors' and the reviewed applications' tops hold as what their find fields find shrinks them.

**Test ids come from the `testid` prefix**: `<testid>-window`, `-submit-button`, `-cancel-button`, `-background`, `-close-button`, `-error`, and what scrolls: `-scroll` when the title and the body scroll together, `-body`, the `-scrolled` content inside it, and `-tray` under `scroll="body"`, and `-shade-top` and `-shade-bottom` on either's shades (a tray's are `-tray-shade-top` and `-tray-shade-bottom`).

**Closing never means saved, and saving never closes.**
The shell emits `submit` and `close` as separate events and never turns one into the other.
Three closes are correct and are pinned: deleting the thing, the explicit close control, and Escape or the backdrop.
The parent learns about a save through its own event, never through `close`: `OrganizationsView` treating close as its refresh signal is why every membership change used to close the dialog.

**A form's submit is one transition table, [`useSubmit`](../../../front-end/src/utils/useSubmit.ts)** (T411): a second press while its request is out sends nothing, and an answer that lands after the form moved on is dropped.
`submitStep` is the table: a form is editing, sending, refused with its sentence, or done, in an opening each answer is tied to.
A submit is taken from any phase but sending; an answer moves only the opening it was sent in; an edit takes a refusal, or a save that stays open, back to editing; and an opening starts editing again, whatever is still out.
`useSubmit({ scope })` holds the table in a component: a change of `scope`'s value is a new opening, taken at once by a synchronous watch, so no answer lands between the change and the opening.
`submit(send, { done, refused, late })` hands the answer to `done`, or the refusal to `refused`, whose returned string is the sentence (else a `Refusal`'s own, else the server's, else the form's `failure`), when it lands in the opening it was sent in, and to `late` when it does not; once the component unmounts no handler is called, `late` included.
A refusal a form makes itself, a write that answered but was not done or one refused before anything is sent, is a [`Refusal`](../../../front-end/src/utils/useSubmit.ts) thrown from `send` with its sentence, so no form needs an error class or a handler of its own to have its sentence said: a verdict someone else changed first, a saved set of table types that adds nothing, a full layout.
A form that must act on more than the sentence extends `Refusal` and reads it in its `refused`, as the vendor's sign-in reads where its code stands (`CodeRefused`).
It reads its state from a plain copy, never the reactive one the screen reads, so a form taken away by its parent's render, as the phase rail takes Open applications, never makes that render track the state and draw again for the write its going makes, which looped the rail without end (T411).
The sign-in pages' forms use it.
The organizer's sign-in ([`LoginView.vue`](../../../front-end/src/views/LoginView.vue), [the accounts flows](../../business/flows/accounts.md)) has one per form, each scoped by the way in, and the code form's by its step too, so Send code and its Sign in share one and "Use a different email" starts another.
So Send code pressed twice sends one request and keeps "code sent", where the server's one-code wait answered the second with a refusal in its place, and a sign-in that lands after the person moved to another way in is dropped: the page neither keeps the address nor moves on.
Each button is disabled while its request is out and says so: "Signing in…", "Creating account…" and "Sending…"; the way-in tabs and "Use a different email" stay enabled, since moving on is allowed and drops the answer.
Each form's line stays the page's own, written from its answer, so the submit's sentence is unused there.
A market's vendor sign-in ([`VendorSignIn.vue`](../../../front-end/src/components/vendorPage/VendorSignIn.vue), [applying online](../../business/flows/applying-online.md)) has one scoped by its step, so Send code, Send a new code and Sign in go through it.
It moves to the code step, and says a refusal, from its own request's answer, which the store's `requestCode` and `verifyCode` return (`{ ok: true }`, or the sentence, where the code stands and whether the server asked for the human check), never from the store's shared `error`, which another reader may write while the request is out.
A request the server answers by asking for the human check is sent once more, with a new token, within the same submit (`withHumanCheck`, [ADR 0045](../../adr/0045-turnstile-is-the-one-human-check.md)).
Until the vendor sends a request it says the store's sentence for a sign-in that ended under them ("Your session has expired..."), which brought them to it.
"Use a different email" is disabled while a request is out, so a late answer never lands on the email step.

**Every dialog that writes on a press holds through its write, by its submission, [`useDialogSubmit`](../../../front-end/src/utils/useDialogSubmit.ts)** (T411, [ADR 0032](../../adr/0032-a-dialogs-write-belongs-to-its-opening.md)), Choose what leads the card aside, which saves each tick as it is made: `useSubmit`'s table keyed by what the dialog is open on, handed to `AppDialog` as `submission`, from which the shell derives the hold, with no prop to remember.
While any one of its submissions sends, Cancel and the close control are drawn in the one disabled state, Escape and the scrim do nothing, and the confirm is disabled; the shell refuses them all, so its parent's `close` handler needs no guard, and a second press sends nothing.
`useDialogSubmit({ opening, failure, says })` takes `opening`, what the dialog is open on: its `open` prop for a dialog component, or the thing its parent opened it with, so opening it on another is a new opening, and a close is one too.
`failure` is the sentence for a refusal with none of its own, and `says` is where the refusal is said: `'dialog'`, the default, by the shell's error line, under the dialog's own `error`, for a single form with one submission; `'self'` by the dialog, beside the field or row it is about, from the handle's `sentence`.
A change to any of a single form's fields (its `input` event) takes a refusal back to editing, so the line goes; a confirmation with no field keeps it until it is pressed again or opened again.
A dialog of several actions hands an array, and holds while any one sends.
**An answer for an earlier opening never touches the one the person is in**: `done` and `refused` hear only the opening the write was sent in, and `late` an earlier one's, which a dialog hands on by its parent's refresh path when its `done` hands the answer to its parent, never by the event that closes, and drops when its parent re-reads on its own; once the dialog unmounts nothing is called.
What a dialog awaits before it closes stays inside its `send`, so the hold lasts as long as that, and a re-read there asks `isCurrent`, which `submit` hands `send`, whether the opening it reads for still stands, so it writes nothing once the dialog has moved on.

**Focus comes back inside when a hold ends, or the confirm is enabled again, with the dialog still open**: the save disabled the confirm that held the focus, which dropped it to `<body>`, and a refusal ends the hold, or enables the confirm (`confirmDisabled` from true to false), with the dialog open, so the shell moves focus in by its `focusInside()`, to its first safe control (below), when focus is on `<body>` or outside the window; focus inside it, or in another dialog opened over it, stays where it is.
The submission's end is the hold's end, and the confirm's end is answered as the hold's is, since a confirm disabled under the focus drops it the same way; a confirm that a field's typing turns on and off leaves the focus in the field, so the rule moves nothing then.
Browser history is not a close and no hold refuses it: it takes the page, dialog and all, so Change reviewers keeps its unanswered write beside the write itself ([`reviewTeam.ts`](../../../front-end/src/utils/reviewTeam.ts)), and a panel drawn before the answer puts the dialog back on it.
A request that never answers is left to a reload: `api` sets no timeout, so the hold lasts as long as the browser's own.

**A destructive dialog opens focused on Cancel**, deliberately: one stray Enter would otherwise be the whole irreversible action, and Cancel is a `type="button"`, so Enter there cancels.
Any other dialog focuses its first enabled control that is not destructive: a dialog listing people never opens on the first person's Remove, which one stray Enter would take (Manage organization did, on its first admin's), and a control that takes something away without being drawn destructive, as a [`SearchPicker.vue`](../../../front-end/src/components/SearchPicker.vue) chip's Remove does, carries `data-dialog-not-first` for the same reason, as does a reviewed row's "Approve instead" or "Reject instead".
**A dialog with no such control opens on a box with more to show, else on its window**, so the arrow keys scroll a read-only list: from the window they would scroll nothing.
What can take focus is one list for the dialog and the box ([`focusable.ts`](../../../front-end/src/utils/focusable.ts)), so a body holding only a fold opens on its summary.
That box is the `ScrollBox` with a Tab stop, which only one with nothing inside it to take focus has, a text entry aside (`fieldsLeaveTheStop`, T223: a find field keeps the arrow keys and brings no row into view, so the Discord report's "Show who", whose find field is its only control until a "Show N more" stands under the rows, opens on the field with the body a Shift+Tab before it), and which it keeps until focus leaves if a control arrives inside it, as the box of Permissions' changes does once they are read, so a dialog whose every control is a chip's Remove, with nothing safe to open on, opens on its window, and Tab reaches the first Remove.
A parent whose control went away with what it did asks for the same again, by the shell's exposed `focusInside()`: Permissions' Reset is disabled once nothing is held, so after a reset focus goes to the dialog's first control ([setting who may do what](../../business/flows/ending-and-managing-a-market.md#l3a-set-who-may-do-what)), and Default permissions does the same ([the default permissions](../../business/flows/organizations.md#b8-set-the-default-permissions-for-new-markets)).
The window draws no ring when it takes focus, as Manage market's does while its market loads: `primitives.css` draws nothing around what only a script focuses ([the design language](../../design-system.md#the-primitive-layer)).
Focus skips `:disabled` controls, because `focus()` on a disabled element is a silent no-op; that left the create-market dialog opening with focus on `<body>` while its organization select loaded.
A modal not on the shell moves focus in itself, and two things it meets: `autofocus` works once a page, which left the floorplan beta's redraw's length field unfocused, and a window hidden by `visibility` cannot take focus until it is shown, so such a window shows at once and only its opacity fades.
**Closing gives focus back to what opened the dialog**, whichever way it closes, and when it is unmounted while open: closing unmounts the window, which dropped focus to `<body>`, so the next Tab started at the top of the page (T45).
The opener is taken as the dialog opens, before focus moves in, and focused once the page behind is no longer inert.
It is left alone when it has left the page, a deleted row or a navigation, and when the parent has already put focus somewhere itself in the same tick.
So a confirmation whose confirm takes its opener away puts focus somewhere itself: Manage organization's and Manage market's Remove asks first (E38/F15/S01), and confirming takes that Remove away with what it removed (the row, or the line of a market role that added nothing), so focus goes to the list it was in, a `tabindex="-1"` group named by its heading, once the confirmation has closed and the page behind is no longer inert; Cancel and Escape leave the row, and focus goes back to its Remove.
Manage market's and Manage organization's Withdraw do the same for an invitation: focus goes to "Invited", or to "Users with access" or Members once none is left (T538).
Manage market's role select asks first too (T211), and the shell gives focus back to the select, by Cancel, Escape, the backdrop, a refusal or a confirm, and the view focuses the select on opening, since Safari does not on a click; only when the re-read leaves no select, as it does for someone who changed their own role, does focus go to that same list.
So an opener that goes away with the click hands focus on first: the phase rail's "More…" menu focuses its button as a choice closes it, as its Escape does, and a Publish or Archive confirm then gives focus back there ([`PhaseRail.vue`](../../../front-end/src/components/PhaseRail.vue)).
`AppDialog` holds no focus trap, so a dialog that swaps one view for another moves focus itself: swapping unmounts the focused button.
So does a row that takes the place of the button that opened it: Manage market's "Invite someone" and Manage organization's "Invite admin" and "Invite member" focus the row's field as it opens, and their button again as its Cancel closes it.
[`useFocusGoesBack`](../../../front-end/src/utils/useFocusGoesBack.ts) holds that, for `AppDialog` and for four modals that are not dialogs on the shell: the [vendor drawer](../../business/flows/vendor-drawer.md) (T123), the door code's full screen, [`SidePanel`](../../design-system.md#the-primitive-layer), the shell proposed for the drawer (T200), and the app bar's sheet.
The drawer moved no focus in and gave none back, so after every vendor a keyboard user started again from the top of the page; it now opens on its close, its first control, since what follows it may be a verdict or a note, and gives focus back to what opened it: a row on the Vendors page or in the review's All view, a seat's name, or a name at the door.
Another vendor shown in the open drawer takes focus to its close the same way: a vendor named in its History tab is shown in place on the Vendors page, and the focused name goes with the panel it sat in, which left focus on `<body>` (T162).

**A field looks like a field.**
A dialog's text input is the `.field` primitive ([the design language](../../design-system.md#the-primitive-layer)), which owns its height, padding, corners, type and disabled state; nothing resets it (`all: unset` once left the market name as bare text).

**Errors sit in the layout.**
A field's error goes beneath its field, in the body; the `error` prop is for one about the submission as a whole and renders in flow above the actions, never positioned over them, with `role="alert"` so a screen reader says it as it arrives.
The invite row's refusal in Manage market, and each invite row's in Manage organization, is brought into view as it arrives, clear of the body's edge shade, since a short window's body can hold that row at its very foot.

**The page behind a modal is out of play, not only dimmed.**
`aria-modal="true"` tells assistive technology that everything outside the dialog is out of play, and that has to be true: the vendor drawer once declared it while fourteen controls behind it stayed in the tab order, the rail's "Publish market" among them, so a keyboard user could publish with a vendor's details open.
A control that can be focused and fired presents itself as available far more strongly than a dimmed one, so the page behind is held `inert` ([`useInertBehind`](../../../front-end/src/utils/useInertBehind.ts)).
**A dialog may mount already open, and is held all the same**: the change-placement dialog is a `v-if` dialog with `:open="true"`, open from setup, before the template ref that names the dialog's root has bound.
`useInertBehind` watched the open state alone, so it walked once with no root to walk from and never again, and Tab walked out of the change-placement dialog onto the market bar behind it (T181).
It watches the dialog's parts with the open state, so the ref binding on mount runs the walk; a re-run releases before it marks, so no element is counted twice, and a branch that a dialog open on top of it holds live stays live (below).
**The page behind holds still** (T473): `inert` takes no scroll, and the scrim is a fixed box with nothing of its own to scroll, so a wheel or a thumb's drag over it scrolled the page behind, at a desk as on a phone, out of play but moving.
While any modal on `useInertBehind` is open, `<html>` carries `holds-still` and [`base.css`](../../../front-end/src/assets/base.css) hides the page's overflow, counted like the marks, so a dialog opened from a dialog closing first leaves the page held for the one beneath.
Hidden overflow keeps the page where it stood, so it is where it was when the last modal closes, and the root's `scrollbar-gutter` is kept under it, so nothing behind moves sideways as the hold comes and goes; what the modal holds still scrolls, in its own `ScrollBox`.
Every modal on `useInertBehind` holds it, the vendor drawer, a `SidePanel`, the app bar's sheet and the door code's full screen with the dialogs, and the hold is taken only with a part on the page, as the marks are.
**On a browser that draws classic scroll bars, the page's bar is gone while a modal holds it, by Daniel's choice (T473)**: the gutter the root keeps shows the page's white beside the scrim, undimmed, as the bar beside the scrim was before; a scrim cannot reach into the gutter, and tinting it to each modal's scrim would not fade in with it.

**A dialog opened from a dialog is a sibling of it**, so [`useInertBehind`](../../../front-end/src/utils/useInertBehind.ts) marked it out of play: it rendered, read correctly, was visible and enabled, and could not be clicked.
A dialog now takes `inert` off its own branch on open and puts the marks back on close; the marks are counted per element, so the dialog beneath still releases correctly, and so is the hold, so a walk of the dialog beneath while the one on top is open leaves the one on top live (T181).
**Escape closes only the dialog on top**: every open dialog took the key, so one press closed a confirmation and the dialog beneath it together and left focus on `<body>` (T114).
[`useEscapeToClose`](../../../front-end/src/utils/useEscapeToClose.ts) keeps the open overlays in the order they opened and hands Escape to the last.
A component inside a dialog that opens a dialog of its own teleports it to the body, so it is that sibling rather than a window inside a window: Manage market's Permissions and its confirmations do ([setting who may do what](../../business/flows/ending-and-managing-a-market.md#l3a-set-who-may-do-what)), as Manage organization's Default permissions and its confirmations do, each placed first in its `Teleport`, since siblings of one layer paint in their order and its confirmations must lie above it.
The vendor drawer's Payment tab teleports its three, Record payment, Change owed and the confirmation its entry and line menus open, in one `Teleport`, as the drawer's Discord offer teleports its confirmation: the drawer's panel carries a `transform`, which makes it the box a fixed descendant is laid out in, so inside it they centred on the panel and not the window (T315).
A dialog opened from inside a sticky block teleports for the same kind of reason: from 1024px [`PinnedTools`](../../../front-end/src/components/PinnedTools.vue)' block is `position: sticky` with a `z-index`, a stacking context, so a dialog left in it paints under the market's frame and the app bar, its scrim leaving both bright; Publish results' confirmation, whose button stands in the review's pinned toolbar, teleports to the body ([`PublishResults.vue`](../../../front-end/src/components/application/PublishResults.vue), T466).
Asserting this needs a control the page holds: the view container is an ancestor of the dialogs and is never marked, so an assertion on it passes whatever the marks say.

## The create-market dialog

Creating a market is a dialog, not the first section of Market Setup, because a market must not exist until the organizer commits to one: creating in place would leave an empty draft behind every abandoned attempt.
It asks only what a market cannot exist without, its organization, its name and how vendors will apply, and lands on the page where everything else is decided.
With exactly one organization it names it rather than offering a select with one answer (`dialog-idiom.spec.ts`); with none it says how to make one (`new-market-org.spec.ts`).

**What Enter does outside a dialog is not decided**: a multi-line text field types a newline and must not submit, and a row editor might reasonably add a row; neither is a dialog.

## Superseded

- The create-market dialog was to ask the name and organization alone (wayfinding/the-order-of-the-work/08); it also asks how vendors will apply, with nothing chosen, since that decides which page it lands on (E31/F03/S01).

## What breaks it

- A hand-rolled overlay with its own scrim, or a key handler for Enter.
- A parent that refreshes on `close`, or a dialog that closes itself as the sign that it saved.
- A component that paints a full-viewport cover without holding the page inert.
- A modal that holds the page behind it some way other than `useInertBehind`, which leaves the page to scroll under its scrim, or a rule that lets `html.holds-still` scroll.
- A dialog that writes with a `busy` and an error of its own and no `submission`: it can be closed while it writes, and the answer lands in its next opening; `everyWritingDialogUsesTheSubmit.test.ts` fails it.
